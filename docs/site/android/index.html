What you need

  • An Android phone with Bluetooth.
  • One compatible radio running Meshtastic.
  • A second, independent compatible radio running MeshCore Companion BLE firmware.

Your phone connects directly to both radios. You do not need the separate touchscreen controller or a special car mount. You choose and provide your radios; Heltec V3 radios and a Samsung S25 are used in development, and other combinations need testing.

Each network keeps its own messages and contacts. MeshCrossway does not forward messages between Meshtastic and MeshCore.

Install. Connect. Explore.

  1. Download the app on your phone. Use the APK download button above. Open the downloaded file and allow installation from your browser or file manager when Android asks.
  2. Allow Bluetooth access. Grant nearby-device and location permissions when requested so the app can find your radios and use GPS features.
  3. Connect your Meshtastic radio. Close any other app connected to that radio. Open Settings in MeshCrossway, select Meshtastic and choose your device.
  4. Connect your MeshCore radio. Repeat for MeshCore with your second radio. Wait for both networks to finish loading.
  5. Try a conversation. Check that each network is ready, send a test message and explore the contacts and map.

Updating an existing app? Install over your current version and choose Update to keep your data. Do not uninstall or clear storage. If you are still using version 0.2.2 or earlier, follow the backup and migration guide first.

New in 0.4.3: support for newer MeshCore repeater replies.

The experimental MeshCore repeater tools can request remote status and up to eight neighbors per page when a compatible repeater is available. Each request requires confirmation, warns about possible flood routing and additional LoRa transmissions, and shares a 30-second manual limit per repeater across status and neighbor pages, including reconnects. There is no automatic polling or retry, and opening a repeater does not transmit anything.

On a real repeater, the first 0.4.1 trial returned an unsupported status format. Version 0.4.2 displays the reply's numeric data length to help diagnose that discrepancy without exposing raw packets. These remote queries passed automated checks but have not yet been validated on every real MeshCore repeater. A radio accepting your request does not guarantee a remote reply. The beta also adds passive dual-network diagnostics, a saved-repeater inventory and clearer Home outbox indicators.

Updating from 0.4.1 or 0.4.0? Back up your local information first, then install the permanently signed 0.4.2 over your existing app without uninstalling. The previous 0.4.1 beta and 0.4.0 beta remain available, but Android normally blocks a direct in-place downgrade to a lower versionCode.

Already in 0.4.0: clearer signals and more reliable messaging.

Each radio now distinguishes live Bluetooth reception from saved signal readings. Open a node to explore a history of reported LoRa RSSI and SNR when the network provides those values. These are measured message details, not guesses about link quality.

Messaging status now separates submitting over Bluetooth, radio acceptance and destination acknowledgements. If a message fails or its delivery is unknown, you can review it and manually prepare a new draft, with a warning about possible duplicates. The app never automatically retransmits an uncertain message.

All the existing channels, links, QR image sharing/scanning, maps, GPS tracks and browser tools remain. Meshtastic and MeshCore still require two independent radios and are never bridged together.

The historical GitHub tag android-v0.4.0 is an older mistakenly numbered build; use android-v0.4.0-stage1 for the previous signed beta.

Heading somewhere without service?

Radio messages do not need cellular service or internet. Download your map areas while online, then check that they open offline before your trip. Delivery depends on your radio setup, terrain and available mesh coverage.

The app is still a beta. Compatibility and reliability are being improved across phones, radios and firmware versions. It should not be your only means of communication in an emergency.
